Section 0

Before you start

Module A replaces the old text-box appraisal with a full KRA / KPI system: masters, goal assignment, three assessment stages, a weighted scoring engine, bands, a bell curve and thirteen reports. This plan walks it end to end in the order you would actually use it.

Everything belongs to a cycle. A performance cycle is the review period. Assessments are only accepted while its status is Open — a Draft cycle takes goals but no scores, and a Locked cycle refuses every change. If something is unexpectedly read-only, check the cycle status first; it is the single most common cause.

Section 2

How a cycle flows

The order matters. Testing out of order will produce empty screens that look like bugs.

  1. Create a cycle (Draft), then Open it. Nothing accepts an assessment until it is Open.
  2. Build the masters — KRAs, then KPIs under each. This is the template, not anyone's goals yet.
  3. Assign goals to people for the cycle. The KPIs are copied onto each person at this moment.
  4. Balance the weightages so every employee totals 100%.
  5. Employee self-assesses in the portal and attaches evidence.
  6. Manager records what was achieved per KPI and rates each KRA.
  7. HR moderates if needed, then finalises — producing the score, band and recommended reward.
  8. HR decides the reward on the Rewards screen, which writes the appraisal record.
  9. Lock the cycle to freeze everything.
What a "send back" does. At any stage a review can be returned. Only the stage you send it to reopens — sending back to the employee leaves the manager's notes intact, and sending back to the manager leaves the employee's self-assessment intact. That asymmetry is deliberate and is tested in cases TC-MGR-06 and TC-HR-05.

Suite 8 · 11 cases

Scoring maths

The arithmetic behind every number on every screen. Check these with a calculator.

How a score is built, in four steps. 1. Each KPI scores 0–100 from target vs achieved.
2. Those roll up into their KRA, weighted by each KPI's own weightage.
3. KRAs roll up into the overall score, weighted by KRA weightage.
4. A KRA with no KPIs is scored from the reviewer's 1–5 rating instead (×20).

Watch the "lower is better" one. Entering 12.5 against a target of 10 should score 80, because 10 ÷ 12.5 = 0.8. Entering 5 — beating the target — should score 100, not 200. That cap is case TC-SCORE-04 and it matters: without it one runaway KPI could push someone into the top band on its own.

Shortcut for the bell curve. Section 9 needs several finalised scores with different values. The quickest way is to assign one KRA (100% weightage, no KPIs) to seven or eight people, rate each of them differently on the Review Desk, and finalise each one. That takes about five minutes and gives you a realistic spread.
